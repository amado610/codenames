# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Groups of friends playing at night in a dim living room. The TV is the shared board and the main light source; two team leaders (spymasters) hold their phones with the secret key. Everyone reads the TV from the couch, 2–4 m away.

## Product Purpose
A Portuguese (pt-PT) Codenames party game: the TV shows 25 words, the leaders' phones show the key and are the only input. Success = the table forgets the tech and plays; the board is readable from the couch and every reveal feels like a moment.

## Brand Personality
1960s spy dossier: classified, tactile, playful-tense. Typewritten index cards, rubber stamps, file folders, a desk lamp in a dark room. Tension on a reveal, a satisfying "slam" when a mission ends.

## Anti-references
Generic neon "cyber" hacker UIs, glassmorphism dashboards, flat Material cards, cartoon party-game look, cream/beige full-page backgrounds.

## Design Principles
- Couch-readable first: TV words must be legible from across the room; decoration never costs legibility.
- The phone is a tool: the spymaster key must be scannable in one glance, big tap targets, no ambiguity about whose turn it is.
- Physical metaphors, not skeuomorphic clutter: cards, stamps and folders carry the theme; everything else stays quiet.
- Motion marks game events (reveal, turn change, victory) and nothing else.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Dark room: avoid pure-white glare on large areas. Team colors must also differ in lightness (not only hue) for color-blind players; revealed state is signalled by more than color. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
